Section 02

How Google's business data actually works

Most of the frustration with Google Business Profile comes from people managing it with a mental model that stopped being true in 2024. Here is the current one.

Correction to my first draft

I originally wrote that the dashboard is gone. That is true for single-location businesses and false for you. Because you run three boutiques, Business Profile Manager at business.google.com/locations is still live and is the correct place for your team to work. I had the multi-location picture wrong when I wrote it; the rest of this section is the corrected version.

Where the profile is edited depends on how many locations you have

Google split the surfaces, which is why advice on this subject contradicts itself so often.

  • Single location — no dashboard. You edit in place: sign in to the owning Google account, search your own name in Google Search or open the listing in Maps, and the owner controls appear on the listing itself.
  • Multiple locations — this is you — business.google.com/locations still exists as Business Profile Manager. It gives you every location in one list, bulk edits, business groups, bulk verification, and proper user roles. None of that is exposed by the Search-and-Maps editing surface.

Practical consequence: if your team has been editing San Jose by searching for it in Maps, they have been using the weaker of the two tools and have no view of the Vietnam profiles at all. Move them to Business Profile Manager and create a business group for the three boutiques.

Bulk chain verification is available at ten or more locations, so at three you will still verify each boutique individually. The group is for management and access control, not verification.

Three levels of access, and they are not equal

RoleCan doCannot do
Primary ownerEverything: edit all fields, add and remove users, transfer ownership, delete the profile—
OwnerEdit all fields, add and remove managersRemove the primary owner; delete the profile
ManagerEdit most fields, post, upload photos, reply to reviewsAdd or remove users; change ownership

First task of this project, before anything else: confirm who holds primary owner. If it is a former employee, an agency you no longer use, or an email nobody can open, every other step here is blocked.

You cannot delete customer photos

You control only the photos uploaded from the business account. Customer uploads — including that crowd shot — can only be reported, and only on policy grounds: irrelevant to the business, copyrighted, offensive, spam, or misleading. Review takes roughly one to seven business days. Google will not remove a photo because you dislike it, and filing the same request repeatedly slows the queue rather than speeding it.

The practical answer

Report what genuinely violates policy, then outnumber the rest. Google surfaces recent, high-quality, high-engagement imagery. A steady stream of good owner photos pushes weak customer uploads down the carousel far more reliably than removal requests do.

Google picks the cover photo, not you

You can nominate a cover and a logo, and Google treats both as strong hints — then its own algorithm decides what actually leads on any given surface and query. You improve your odds with an image that is high resolution, genuinely representative, uncluttered in the background, weighted toward the top half of the frame, and — critically — original. Google now runs duplicate and copyright checks against uploads, so an image already published elsewhere is a weaker candidate than a fresh file.

The fields most businesses never fill

  • Services — each one listed individually with its own short description. A store listed only as "Jewelry store" does not surface for ring resizing near me or jewelry appraisal san jose, which are far less competitive queries than "jewelry store."
  • Attributes — the filter checkboxes: identifies as Asian-owned, by appointment, in-store shopping, wheelchair accessible, offers custom orders. These feed Maps' filtering UI.
  • Social links — Google now supports Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn, Pinterest, TikTok, X, and YouTube. One URL per platform, live within minutes to 72 hours.
  • Special hours — holiday overrides. For this business Tết matters more than any federal holiday, and Google surfaces holiday hours prominently.
  • Products — a native catalogue, or a Merchant Center feed for the full Shopify inventory. You list over 1,200 products and advertise 100,000+ certified diamonds; almost none of that is reachable from Maps today.

Q&A is being replaced by an AI layer, and it raises the stakes on everything above

The old customer Q&A panel is giving way to Ask Maps, which answers a visitor's questions in real time by reading your profile rather than waiting for a human to reply. You cannot write its answers directly. What you can do is control what it reads.

Why the boring fields suddenly matter more

Every empty field used to cost you a filter match. Now it costs you an answer. When someone asks Maps "do they do ring resizing?" or "is there parking?" or "do they speak Vietnamese?", the reply is assembled from your categories, services, attributes, and description. A thin profile does not merely rank poorly — it produces vague or wrong answers to questions from people already trying to buy. The services list in Section 07 is the highest-leverage unglamorous hour on this whole project.

Section 03

Why your edits disappear

This is the part that makes people give up, so it is worth understanding before you start typing.

Google does not treat your edit as truth. It treats it as one claim among several and reconciles it against your website, third-party directories, aggregator feeds, and public "suggest an edit" submissions from Maps users. When those disagree, the edit sits in review — usually minutes, occasionally up to thirty days — and may be quietly rejected or reverted.

SymptomReal causeFix
Edit stuck "pending" Name, address, or category changes trigger a deeper review cycle than hours or description Change one high-risk field at a time; leave a week between them
Hours revert Conflicting hours published on Yelp and other directories outweigh a single unsupported edit Correct the directories first, then Google — Section 08, Phase 4
Data reappears after deletion A listing-management tool, an old agency account, or a POS/booking integration is still syncing stale records Audit every connected service; disconnect the ones nobody owns
Nothing saves at all Profile is unverified or mid-verification (postcard, email, or video) Complete verification before touching anything else
Photos vanish after upload Policy rejection — watermark, overlaid text, phone number, large logo, stock imagery, or a duplicate of an image found elsewhere Re-export clean originals per Section 04
The rule that follows from all of this

Fix the sources, then the profile. Editing Google while Yelp, Apple Maps, Bing, Facebook, and your own website still publish the old address, hours, and phone is how a profile ends up reverting for months. The order in Section 08 is deliberate.

A note on the suite number

You are inside Grand Century Mall, and 1111 Story Rd is shared by dozens of businesses. Suite 1063 is what distinguishes you — it is also the number visible on your own storefront in the current lead photo. Format it identically in every single place: Google, the website, Yelp, Apple Maps, Bing, invoices. Mall addresses are a common source of duplicate and merged listings, so while you are in there, search Maps for your own name and confirm there is exactly one Hung Phat USA listing. If a duplicate exists, report it for merge rather than editing it into agreement.

What gets an image rejected

  • Overlaid text beyond roughly 10% of the frame. Phone numbers and large logos are auto-rejected.
  • Watermarks, especially another company's branding. A small, subtle corner mark sometimes survives, but it is a coin flip and not worth the risk on a hero image.
  • Stock photography. Purchased or licensed stock is explicitly disallowed and may be removed.
  • Heavy filters or significant alteration. Google's standard is that the image represents reality — in focus, well lit, not stylised into fiction.
  • Duplicates. Google runs duplicate and copyright checks. An image already all over your Instagram is a weaker upload than a fresh export from the same shoot.
  • Promotional banners, borders, and collages. These read as advertisements, not photographs.
This is the one that will bite you

Jewellery marketing assets are almost always built with a logo lockup, a price, or a promotional line baked in. Those are exactly the files that get rejected. Google needs the un-branded originals — the frame before the graphic designer touched it. Budget for pulling raw exports out of the archive, not repurposing finished ads.

How many, and how often

Google's own floor is three photos per category — three exterior, three interior, three team. That is a floor, not a target. Given that top-ranked local results carry hundreds of images, plan for an initial set of 25–35 strong files and then a sustained drip. Recency is itself a signal, so 3–5 new photos per week beats thirty uploaded in one afternoon and then silence for a year.

Section 05

The look: one visual system, three surfaces

This is the consistency spec you asked for. It is written as rules rather than as a set of picked files — I still cannot see your photographs. But rules are what make a set consistent anyway: files chosen by taste drift, files chosen against a written spec do not.

You already wrote the art direction. It is sitting on your About page.

The most valuable thing the research turned up is not a Google specification. It is your own copy, and it is far better than anything I would have written for you:

hungphatusa.com/pages/about-us — "Three generations, one quiet bench."

"It started with a small bench in Vietnam, a loupe, and a father teaching his son how to hold a stone to the light. Many decades and an ocean later — the bench has moved. The work has not."

"We did not set out to be the largest jeweler. We set out to be your jeweler — the one your sister visits before a proposal, the one your mother trusts with the pieces she loves, the one your daughter will visit when it is her turn."

"Every piece is designed, cast, set, and finished under our own roof — by jewelers we trained, to a standard we set."

That copy makes the visual direction almost self-selecting, and it resolves the question you actually asked. Consistency is not a colour palette you apply to Google. It is your existing story, told in photographs. Three commitments follow from it directly:

  • The bench is the hero, not the product. Your story is about a bench, hands, and a standard — so the photographs that carry the brand are the workshop, the loupe, the setter's hands. Frame 10 in the shot list is not a nice-to-have; it is the literal subject of your About page, and it is entirely absent from Google today.
  • Quiet, not loud. "One quiet bench" rules out the whole vocabulary of discount jewellery marketing — starbursts, price flashes, sparkle overlays, black velvet drama. It also happens to rule out precisely what Google rejects. Your brand voice and Google's photo policy want the same thing, which almost never happens.
  • People, across generations. "The one your sister visits before a proposal… the one your daughter will visit when it is her turn" is a promise about relationships. Team and consultation photographs are not filler in this set — they are the proof of the central claim.

Your homepage line — "Your jeweler for life" — is the sentence every image in the set should be able to support. If a candidate photograph could equally belong to a mall chain running a diamond sale, it fails, regardless of how good it looks.

The material logic

Your brand's distinguishing materials are warm gold, jade, and lacquer red — that is what the storefront arch already says, and it is what separates you from every chain jeweller shooting on black velvet. The photography should be built around making those three read true, and everything else in the spec follows from that.

VariableThe ruleWhy
White balance Lock to 4800–5200 K across the entire set. One value, every frame. Your current entrance photo has warm gold signage fighting cold blue case lighting in the same frame. Mixed temperature is the single fastest way to make a set look unplanned.
Key light Soft front-left key, plus one hard specular kicker for stones. Soft light renders gold and jade accurately; you need the hard source separately or diamonds go flat and grey.
Grounds Exactly two: a warm ivory sweep for product, and the real showroom for everything else. Two grounds reads as a system. Five reads as a folder. Skip black velvet — it disappears in a small Maps tile and reads as generic.
Framing Shoot 4:3 or 3:2, compose so a centre-square crop still works. Subject in the middle third. You do not control the crop on Tesla, CarPlay, or the Maps sheet.
Weighting On exterior and cover candidates, put the signal in the top half of the frame. Google's cover selection favours it, and bottom-crop is the most common trim.
Post-processing Exposure, white balance, lens correction, light dust cleanup. Nothing else. Filters and heavy retouching trip the "significant alteration" rule.
Graphics Zero. No logo, no text, no border, no price. Rejection risk, and it makes a photo read as an ad.
People Staff yes, with a signed release. Customers only with written permission. Team photos are one of Google's three recommended categories, and they convert.

The selection rubric — run this on your existing library

You said you have a lot of good images. Most of them are probably good for Instagram and wrong for Google, which rewards a different thing entirely. Score each candidate; anything below 5 does not go on Google.

  • +1 — It survives a centre-square crop with the subject intact.
  • +1 — It is a genuine, un-retouched-looking photograph of this store, these people, or these pieces.
  • +1 — No text, logo, watermark, border, or price anywhere in the frame.
  • +1 — White balance sits in the locked range and gold reads gold, not orange or green.
  • +1 — Long edge is at least 1600 px, file is between 10 KB and 5 MB.
  • +1 — It shows something a customer needs to know: where the door is, what the room feels like, who will serve them, what the work looks like.
  • −2 — It is already published on Instagram or the website as the identical file. Re-export a different frame from the same shoot instead.
  • −3 — Anything dated: COVID signage, expired promotions, retired displays, old fit-out.
  • Instant fail, regardless of score — it could belong to a mall chain running a diamond sale. That is the "quiet bench" test, and it is the one that keeps the set yours.

Section 08

Rollout, in order

The sequence matters more than the speed. Doing phase 4 before phase 2 is how edits revert.

Phase 0Half a day
Access, ownership, and the baseline

Sign in to Business Profile Manager at business.google.com/locations — not Maps — and create a business group for the three boutiques. Establish who holds primary owner. Add two current staff as owners so the business is never locked out again. Remove former employees and dormant agency accounts. Confirm verification is complete; nothing saves reliably until it is.

Export the Performance report the same day. Google keeps only a rolling six months and then deletes it permanently. Every day you wait, a month of the pre-project baseline is gone for good — and without it you cannot prove any of this worked.

Blocker check: everything downstream depends on ownership. If the primary owner is unreachable, the reclaim process runs on Google's timeline, not yours — start it today.

Phase 1Week 1
Decide the canonical facts

One primary phone. One set of hours. One Instagram account. One address format including the suite. One wording of the 3D design offer. Confirm 1982 / 2001 / 2012. Write them down in a single document that every staff member and vendor works from — this is the reference every later phase copies from, in both countries.

While you are here: search Maps for your own name and confirm there is exactly one San Jose listing. If duplicates exist, merge them carefully — reviews transfer, but owner replies frequently do not, and Google processes one merge at a time. With a 5.0 rating you have a lot to lose in a careless merge.

Phase 2Week 1
Text fields on Google

Categories, description, services, attributes, hours, special hours, social links, UTM-tagged website. Change high-risk fields — name, address, primary category — one at a time with days between them. In the same session, report the genuinely policy-violating customer photos.

Phase 3Week 2
The shoot

Half a day, Section 06 shot list, Section 05 look spec. Set-dress first. Deliver un-branded originals at the sizes in Section 04, plus square and vertical crops for Instagram from the same frames.

Phase 4Week 2–3
Directories, then the drip

Publish the canonical facts to Yelp, Apple Maps, Bing Places, Facebook, the BBB, and the chamber listing — identical strings everywhere. Then begin uploading photos to Google at 3–5 per week rather than all at once. Set the cover and logo early in the sequence so Google has time to consider them.

Phase 5Week 3–4
The website side

Add the JSON-LD block. Build or refresh a Visit page carrying the same shoot, the same hours, an embedded map, and the suite number. Validate with the Rich Results Test.

Phase 6Month 2
Put the catalogue on the map

You sell 1,200+ products and advertise 100,000+ certified diamonds, and almost none of it is reachable from a Maps listing. Link Merchant Center to the profile and turn on free local listings, which put in-stock items in front of nearby shoppers at no media cost. If you run Shopify POS with real inventory, pickup today becomes available on top of it.

Treat this as its own project rather than a checkbox — feed quality is the whole game, and jewellery feeds are fussy about GTINs and product identifiers. Start with the strongest few hundred SKUs, not all 1,200.

Phase 7Ongoing
Keep it alive

One Google post per week. Three to five new photos per week. Reply to every review inside 48 hours — responses are read by most customers before they buy, and they are a ranking input. Re-check hours before every holiday, Tết first. Export the Performance report monthly so you are never again at the mercy of the six-month window.

What to measure

The Performance report — Google renamed it from Insights, which is why half the internet still calls it the wrong thing — gives you views, calls, direction requests, website clicks, messages, bookings, and, since March 2026, offers. Views count unique visitors once per day. The searches breakdown refreshes at the start of each month and can lag five days.

The number to watch is direction requests: it is the closest available proxy for someone actually driving to Story Road, and photo work moves it faster than anything else on this list. Compare against the baseline you exported in Phase 0 — and remember that the baseline only exists if somebody actually exports it, because Google will delete it.
