◆ The finding that reframes everything

The cheapest quotes you are about to receive cannot legally do this job — and that fact, not any star rating, is what sorts this market.

Since 1 January 2025, an unlicensed person in California may take on a project only if the total price — labour, materials, everything — is under $1,000, no building permit is required, and they hire nobody to help. A residential sprinkler install starts at roughly $590–$2,000 per zone and a typical yard needs 3 to 5 zones. Even the smallest realistic job is several times over the line, and a two-person crew breaches the "no helper" condition on day one.

So the lowest bids in this category — the gardener with a trencher, the crew from a neighbourhood flyer — are not merely a cheaper option with more risk. They are outside the law for work of this size, and hiring one strips you of the $25,000 bond, of CSLB complaint jurisdiction, and of the workers'-comp shield that stops a trenching injury in your yard from landing on your homeowner's policy. That is why this report ranks on licence class, licence status and water-compliance competence first, and on price second. It is also why the price gap you'll see between bids is mostly not negotiating room — it's a legality gap.

◆ The payment rule that protects you

Whatever the total, your deposit is capped at $1,000 or 10% of the contract price — whichever is less. On a $6,000 job that's $600, not $1,000. This is not negotiable and not waivable; a contractor asking for "half up front" is proposing something California law forbids, and the contract must print the cap in 12-point bold type. Treat a request to exceed it as a complete answer about who you're dealing with.

Warranty terms — what "good" looks like

The industry standard is one year, parts and labour. Better contractors offer 2–3 years on parts and sometimes 2 years on labour. The distinction that matters: a "1-year parts only" warranty is close to worthless, because on a typical valve replacement the labour is the bill. Separately, the manufacturers carry their own trade warranties — Rain Bird warrants several core lines (1800 spray heads, 5000/5500 rotors, PEB/PESB valves) for 5 years, with valves, rotors and controllers generally in a 2–10 year band; Hunter valves typically run 2–5 years from manufacture. So a contractor offering "1 year parts" is offering you less than the parts already carry. Ask for labour cover, in years, in writing.

Source: Rain Bird Professional Customer Satisfaction Policy & Trade Warranty · rainbird.com; Hunter warranty terms · read 15 Aug 2026 Search-derived (Manufacturers' own published warranty policies — primary and binding on them. Rain Bird and Hunter are the two dominant irrigation-equipment makers in the US residential market.)

Third-party risk

  • Subcontracted trenching crews. The most common structure in this trade is a licensed principal plus day labour. If the crew isn't covered by workers' comp, an injury on your property can reach your homeowner's policy. Ask, in writing, whether every person on site is covered.
  • Supplier liens. Materials for a 4-zone system come from a supply house on credit. If your contractor doesn't pay them, the supplier can lien your home even though you paid the contractor in full. This is the specific risk the release process below neutralises.
  • Rebate dependency. If your bid assumes a Valley Water rebate, you're exposed to a programme with a finite annual budget and an approved-equipment list. Get pre-approval before the equipment is ordered, not after.

Your contract protections — the five to insist on

  • Deposit at the legal cap or below — $1,000 or 10%, whichever is less, printed in the contract.
  • Conditional lien release before each progress payment, unconditional release after it clears — from the contractor and from any supplier or sub who served you a preliminary notice. You may lawfully withhold the next payment until the previous unconditional releases arrive. Use it.
  • Warranty stated in years, separately for parts and labour, plus a named response time for callbacks.
  • A certificate of insurance sent to you directly by their broker — never a PDF forwarded by the contractor. This is the single highest-value item in the §10 emails; a forwarded certificate is trivially stale or edited, one from the broker is not.
  • An as-built zone map and the controller programme, handed over on completion. Buried pipe you have no map of is a liability for the next twenty years, and for whoever repairs it.

09 Assessment

My assessment, in plain English

No jargon. Short sentences.

The situation, simply

You want sprinklers put in at a house in Silver Creek. There are plenty of people who will do it. The trouble is that the ones who quote cheapest mostly aren't allowed to. California moved the line on 1 January 2025: no licence means no job over $1,000, materials included. Your job is worth several thousand. So a whole tier of bidders is out before you compare anything.

That leaves a smaller field of licensed firms. I found five that serve your area. I could confirm licence numbers for three of them. I could not read a single customer review, because this machine cannot open Yelp, Google or the BBB. I am not going to pretend otherwise, and I have not made any up.

Why your priority beats price

You didn't tell me what matters most, so I chose: a licence you can check, and someone who understands water. Here's why that beats saving a few hundred dollars.

Pipe goes underground. Once the trench is filled you cannot see the work. If the heads are spaced wrong or the pressure is off, you don't find out for months — you find out from brown patches and a water bill. Fixing it means digging it up again. That's the whole job twice.

And your lot slopes. Water pressure drops as pipe runs uphill. Someone who doesn't calculate for that will hand you a system that waters the bottom of the garden well and the top badly. It will look fine on day one.

The recommendation

Cyprex Construction Landscapes. The headline reason: they are the only one who wrote down how they design a system — pressure, flow, friction, elevation. On a sloped lot, that's the whole ball game.

Three more reasons, in order:

  • They hold three licence classes, not one. Trenching, grading and drainage stay in-house. Fewer strangers on your property, and fewer people who could lien your house.
  • Thirty-one years. Long enough that mistakes would have caught up with them.

The honest catch: they're a design-build firm. They may be dearer, and they may want to design more than you asked for. Ask on the first call whether they'll quote a straightforward sprinkler install without a landscape redesign attached. If the answer is no, go to the runner-up without regret.

Dooling Landscape Service. Forty-two years in business and the only licence with a clear expiry date I could see. The owner has a horticulture degree. If Cyprex is too big or too slow for what you want, call Dooling.

Why I chose them

Because on a sloped Silver Creek lot the risk isn't a bad company — it's a competent company that doesn't do the arithmetic. Elevation change eats pressure, and pressure loss shows up as uneven coverage you cannot see until the grass tells you months later, when the pipe is buried and the remedy is the job again. Cyprex is the only candidate whose own material names the four variables that govern this — precipitation rate, flow, friction loss, head elevation. That got the heaviest weight I gave anything. It is only marketing copy, and I've said so, but it is marketing copy that the other four didn't write, and in a field where everyone lists the same services, knowing the vocabulary is the closest available proxy for knowing the work.

Second in weight, the licence stack: A and B alongside C-27 means grading, drainage and trenching stay in-house instead of going to a crew you never met — which cuts both the workers'-comp exposure and the supplier-lien exposure set out in §07. Third, and much lighter, thirty-one years and the Native Plant Society listing: longevity that would have caught up with them by now if the work were poor, and a signal they can actually get your rebate approved. I gave the review platforms almost no weight at all — not because they don't matter, but because I couldn't read them, and pretending otherwise would be the one thing that makes a report like this worthless.
