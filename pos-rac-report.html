The reframe — see below
◆ The finding that reframes everything

Your Square receipt "problem" is a settings limitation on a requirement that no longer exists.

Square can print a signature-and-tip line — but two of its own rules hide it: "Sign on printed receipt" is unavailable for contactless (tap) payments entirely, and it's also disabled whenever "Ask for tips before payment" is on — which is the default. So a stock Terminal, taking tap payments, prints no signature line at all. It's a configuration constraint, not a broken product.

More importantly: the card networks eliminated the signature requirement for card-present transactions in April 2018. A signature line is now optional and carries no network mandate. So the feature that started this search is largely obsolete — which means the decision should turn on what you actually pay every month, not on a signature box. That single shift is what moves Helcim ahead of everyone.

If it locks me into a multi-year contract, or hides its real rate behind a reseller, it's out. You are leaving Square precisely to pay less and stay flexible. 07 The fine print

The policy layer

The governing rule, the vendor terms side by side, third-party risk, warranty, and what actually protects you.

The governing law — and what you lose if it goes wrong

The rule that reshaped this whole decision is the card networks' removal of the signature Cardholder-Verification requirement, effective 14 April 2018 (Visa, Mastercard, American Express, Discover). Since that date, EMV-enabled US/Canada merchants may stop capturing signatures for any amount and need not retain signed receipts. What you lose if you get this wrong: nothing operationally — but you'd be choosing a POS to solve a problem that regulation already dissolved, and possibly paying more per transaction for a signature box you don't need.

The second governing framework is PCI DSS (the card-industry data-security standard). All five vendors maintain PCI-compliant software; the risk is a PCI non-validation fee if you don't complete your annual self-attestation. Square and Helcim charge no PCI fee; Clover may bill a non-validation fee. What you lose if you get this wrong: monthly non-compliance charges and, in a breach, liability for fines.

Third-party risk

The biggest third-party risk in this category is the reseller/ISO layer — most acute for Clover, whose 3,000+ resellers set their own contracts and can bundle non-cancelable hardware leases. Square, Helcim and Stripe sell direct, largely removing this. Warranty & your contract protections

Hardware warranty

Square: 1-year limited (return defective unit within 14 days of replacement). Clover: 1-year, or fuller Equipment Protection Program with a subscription. Helcim/Stripe/Toast: standard manufacturer warranty on purchased hardware. Buy hardware outright where possible to avoid lease traps.

Your protections to demand in writing

(1) A rate lock or written cap on rate increases; (2) no early-termination fee in the signed agreement; (3) confirmation of no monthly minimum and no PCI fee; (4) the deposit-timing SLA in writing; (5) their PCI DSS Attestation of Compliance on request.

09 No jargon

My assessment, in plain English

The situation, simply

You're on Square. It works, but it costs more per sale than it needs to, and its receipt won't print a signature line the way you want. You asked for something cheaper with a signature line and a lower monthly fee.

Why your priority beats price alone

Here's the twist: the signature line barely matters anymore — the card companies stopped requiring signatures in 2018. So the thing worth chasing is the per-sale fee. At $120,000 a month, even half a percent is real money — about $8,000 a year. Your priority isn't "a signature box," it's "stop overpaying." That's what should drive the choice.

The recommendation

Go with Helcim. One headline reason: at your sales volume it charges the lowest true rate of anyone here, with no monthly fee and no contract. Three more, ranked:

  • You keep your freedom. Month-to-month, no cancellation fee — if it disappoints, you leave. That protects the whole decision.
  • The quiet costs go away. No 1.95% fee to get your money fast; refunded sales give the fee back.
  • It still does the receipt. Tips and a signature line are built in — your original complaint is answered anyway.

The honest catch: Helcim is smaller than Square with fewer reviews, and its exact rate depends on your card mix — so the $8,000 is a solid estimate, not a guarantee. Run one month and check the statement.

The runner-up

Toast — but only if you're a restaurant. It has the lowest sticker rate and the best signature/tip receipt, yet it ties you to a multi-year contract with an exit fee, and it doesn't fit retail or service businesses. Great tool, wrong shape unless you serve food.

Who you didn't pick, and why

Square — fine product, just priced above cost-plus; you're leaving it for a reason. Stripe — cheap and flexible but needs a developer to set up; not turnkey. Clover — capable hardware, but you can't even learn your real price without signing with a reseller, and those deals lock you in. That opacity is the dealbreaker.

The uncomfortable part you should know

Every processor in this category — Square, Helcim, Stripe, Clover — has complaints about sudden account holds and frozen funds. Switching to save money doesn't remove that risk; it's baked into the industry. Keep a cash buffer and don't route 100% of your income through a single new processor on day one.

What I'd actually do, in order

  • 1 · Email Helcim and Toast for written, itemized quotes (templates in §10).
  • 2 · Ask Helcim, in writing, for your exact effective rate at $120k/mo and confirmation of no monthly/PCI/cancellation fees.
  • 3 · Pull one month of your Square statements and count your true card mix (debit vs credit) and average ticket.
  • 4 · Run Helcim in parallel for one month on part of your volume; compare the real effective rate to Square.
  • 5 · If Helcim's live rate lands under ~2.4%, move fully and keep Square as a dormant backup for 60 days.

The final verdict — the closing argument

Go with Helcim.

Open a Helcim account, request your written effective-rate quote at $120k/month, and run it in parallel with Square for 30 days on part of your volume — then move fully once the live rate lands under ~2.4%.

Why I chose it. Your stated priority is to stop overpaying on per-transaction fees, and once the signature-line issue collapsed — the card networks killed that requirement in 2018, and every alternative prints one anyway — the decision became purely about cost and freedom. Helcim wins both. Its interchange-plus model puts your effective rate near or below 2% versus Square's ~2.9–3.0%, a modeled ~$8,000/year, and it does so with no monthly fee, no contract and no cancellation fee — so the saving comes with an easy exit if it disappoints. I weighted the cost evidence most heavily (it's the biggest, most durable number and it's grounded in a published rate structure), the contract flexibility second (it protects the whole decision), and the receipt/tip parity third (it neutralizes your original complaint at no cost). The one thing I discounted: headline review counts — Helcim's smaller base reflects its size, not a defect.

Why I eliminated each of the others

Square
Genuinely good — turnkey, transparent, no contract, beloved on G2/Capterra. Knocked out because it's priced above cost-plus at your volume and carries two quiet costs (1.95% instant transfer, refund-fee retention). Stays as 60-day backup
Toast
Lowest sticker rate and the best signature/tip receipt in the field. Knocked out by a multi-year contract with an early-termination fee and a restaurant-only fit. Back-up if you're a restaurant
Stripe
Contract-free, transparent, lowest per-transaction cents. Knocked out because it isn't turnkey — it needs developer work to stand up signature/tip capture. Back-up only for custom builds
Clover
Capable hardware with strong native tip/signature. Knocked out because you can't learn your real price or contract without signing through a reseller, and those deals commonly lock you in — a direct violation of your elimination rule. Not retained

What would change my mind

One fact: if your Square statements show a rewards-credit-heavy card mix that pushes Helcim's live effective rate above ~2.6%, the saving evaporates — and I'd flip to Toast if you're a restaurant (its negotiated flat 2.49% would then win despite the contract), or simply stay on Square if you're retail and value its turnkey simplicity. That's why step one is a real quote and a one-month parallel run, not an immediate full switch.
