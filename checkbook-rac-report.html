↺ The reframe — the box is the cheap part

Buying checks is a fraud decision wearing a stationery decision's clothes.

The entire spread between the most expensive and cheapest place to buy 250 business checks is roughly $100. That's real, and this report will get it for you. But the same 250 checks put your routing number, account number and signature into circulation on paper, into the mail stream, and into a printer's order database — and check fraud is the one fraud category that is growing while check usage falls. B2B check use is at an all-time low of 33%, yet checks remain the payment type most often hit by fraud (§22, §24).

So the correct order is: buy the smallest quantity that covers a year, on high-security stock, from a known printer, and spend the money you saved on positive pay at your bank. Then move every recurring vendor you can onto ACH inside QuickBooks — at $2–4 per check issued versus 26–50¢ per ACH, the payment method costs more than the paper ever will.

02 Decide this first

What you're actually buying

Four questions decide your vendor. Answer them before you open a single checkout page — the wrong format is a $200 mistake nobody refunds.

1 · Hand-written or printed from QuickBooks?

This is the fork in the road. Hand-written means a bound book — 3-on-a-page business checks with side-tear stubs in a 7-ring binder, or a desk/wallet book. Printed means laser voucher checks (one check plus two detachable stubs, letter-sized) that feed through your office printer and are filled in by QuickBooks. Since your books already run in QuickBooks, voucher stock is the format that keeps your check register reconciled without double entry. Most businesses end up buying both: a small hand-written book for one-offs, a box of voucher stock for AP runs. Structural — not price-dependent

2 · QuickBooks Online or Desktop?

They print to different layouts, and check stock is not returnable once it carries your account number. Order stock explicitly sold as compatible with your version — Deluxe, Costco, Sam's Club, Checks In The Mail and Checks Unlimited all filter their catalogue by software compatibility. If you buy the wrong one, the amount lands in the wrong band and every check in the box is scrap. Search-derived

Source: Intuit — "Print checks on blank check stock" · quickbooks.intuit.com/learn-support/en-us/help-article/print-file/print-checks-blank-check-stock · read 19 Aug 2026 (Intuit's own help article — authoritative for what QuickBooks can print and on what stock. Search-derived; direct page load was egress-blocked.)

3 · Pre-printed, or blank high-security stock?

Pre-printed means your bank details are printed at the factory. Blank stock means you buy security paper with no account data on it and your software prints the bank line, the MICR line and the amount in one pass. Blank stock is dramatically cheaper per sheet at volume — roughly $20–50 per 250 versus $50–80 per 250 pre-printed, and about 1,000 blanks for the price of 250 pre-printed — and one box serves every bank account you hold. The costs are a printer setup, MICR toner if you want the highest teller-acceptance rate, and the fact that blank stock in a supply closet is worthless to a thief while a pre-printed box is not. Search-derived

4 · How fast do you need them?

Normal delivery from any printer is roughly 1–2 weeks on free standard shipping; rush is available everywhere and costs more than the checks. If you need to write a check today, that isn't a purchasing problem — walk into your branch and ask for counter checks, printed on the spot against your account, typically $1–5 each and issued in small sheets. Don't buy an expensive rush box to solve a two-check emergency. Search-derived

▲ The rule that eliminates vendors

If the checkout hides a handling fee, pre-checks a monitoring subscription, or the printer isn't one your bank sees every day, it's out. Ordering checks means handing a stranger your routing number, account number, business name and address — the exact package needed to counterfeit a check. A vendor whose own checkout is designed to extract an extra $9 from you without consent has told you how it treats your data. This rule is what demotes Checks In The Mail and Checks Unlimited despite genuinely low sticker prices, and it's why "cheapest search result" is the wrong way to pick.

⚖ How to read a 1.4-star check printer

Nobody reviews a check order that arrives on time — the product is boring and the purchase is once every two or three years. Review pages for this category collect almost exclusively the failures: the late box, the wrong account number, the subscription nobody noticed. That's why Checks Unlimited holds an A+ at the BBB and a 1.5 at Trustpilot simultaneously — one measures whether complaints get resolved, the other collects the complaints. The useful signal isn't the star count; it's what the complaints are about. Late deliveries are annoying. A pre-checked subscription and a surprise handling fee are a business-model choice, and that's the signal I acted on.

07 The fine print

The fraud layer — the part that actually matters

Every dollar of the purchase decision is smaller than one washed check. Here's the current threat picture and the controls that answer it.

What's happening right now

  • 63% of financial institutions reported check-fraud attempts in the prior 12 months; 32% saw more counterfeit checks, 21% more check washing, 18% more payee forgery.
  • 79% of U.S. organizations faced payment-fraud attempts in 2024, and 63% of those incidents involved checks; 58% of organizations reported check fraud in 2025 — still the most-hit payment type, ahead of ACH and wire.
  • Mail-theft-driven check fraud was estimated at $21 billion in 2023, with a large majority of incidents never reported. Postal inspectors report intercepting over $1 billion in fraudulent checks and money orders annually.
  • Check washing is low-tech and effective: a stolen check, a solvent, a new payee and a new amount.
Sources: Federal Reserve Financial Services — 2026 Risk Officer Report · frbservices.org · AFP 2026 Payments Fraud & Control Survey · financialprofessionals.org · FinCEN Financial Trend Analysis on mail-theft check fraud · fincen.gov · read 19 Aug 2026 (Regulator and industry-body primary research — the strongest available evidence on check-fraud direction. Search-derived; direct loads were egress-blocked.)

Demand these on the stock you order

  • Chemically sensitive paper that stains visibly when solvents touch it
  • Microprint border — blurs on photocopy or scan
  • Invisible fluorescent fibers
  • Erasure protection and a security screen with warning box
  • Void pantograph that surfaces "VOID" on a copy
  • Hologram tier if a single check can move serious money

Controls that beat any paper

  • Positive pay — you send the bank your issued-check file; anything presented that doesn't match on check number, payee or amount is flagged for you to approve or reject before it pays
  • Restrict signing authority to named signers; lock up any signature stamp or plate
  • Store unused stock in a locked cabinet and log the check-number ranges
  • Never leave outgoing checks in a residential mailbox or a blue collection box — hand them to a clerk
  • Write in permanent gel ink; never sign a blank check
  • Reconcile in QuickBooks against the bank feed daily, not monthly — the window to report an altered check is short
▲ The one call to make this week

Ask your bank what positive pay costs on your business checking account and turn it on. It is the single control that converts check fraud from a loss you discover later into a transaction you decline in advance. Pair it with ACH-first payables in QuickBooks and the checkbook becomes what it should be: an exception tool, not a payment system.

09 No jargon

My assessment, in plain English

You asked where to buy checkbooks. The short answer: Costco, or Sam's Club if that's the card in your wallet. Both print through the same industrial check printers your bank uses, both cost roughly half what the bank charges, and shipping is free. Order the smallest quantity that covers a year or so.

Before you order, check one thing: whether your business checking account already includes free checks. Some account tiers do. If yours does, the cheapest place to buy checks is the one you're already paying for.

Two formats, and you probably want both. A 3-on-a-page business book with stubs for checks you write by hand, and laser voucher checks that print out of QuickBooks for anything you're paying from your books. Match the voucher stock to your QuickBooks version — Online and Desktop print differently, and the wrong box is unusable.

Skip the deep-discount check mailers unless you have a working coupon and the patience to read the order summary line by line. Their advertised prices are real; the totals often aren't, and one of them pre-selects a paid monitoring subscription at checkout.

If you need a check today, don't pay for rush shipping — walk into your branch and ask for counter checks. A few dollars each, printed while you wait.

And the thing I'd want you to take away even if you ignore the rest: the checkbook is the cheap part. Every check you write costs a few dollars all-in and lands in the payment category criminals target hardest right now. Call your bank about positive pay, and move your recurring vendors onto ACH in QuickBooks. That's worth more than the entire price difference in this report, every year.

Final verdict

Order from Costco Checks. Order small. Then call your bank about positive pay.

Place one order: a 250-check 3-on-a-page business book with stubs, plus voucher stock matched to your QuickBooks version. Confirm the high-security line at checkout, continue your existing check-number sequence, and decline every add-on.

Why I chose it. You asked a sourcing question, and on sourcing the answer is unambiguous: the warehouse clubs print through the same industrial check printers the banks use, at roughly half of bank pricing and below the general printer market, with free standard shipping and business formats that cover both of your use cases. Costco edges Sam's Club only because its price points are the better-corroborated of the two — if your wallet holds the other card, take the other card and change nothing else. I weighted three things: verifiable price (the biggest, most durable gap), checkout integrity (a vendor that pre-selects a subscription will do worse things with your account number), and business-format coverage including QuickBooks compatibility. I discounted star ratings almost entirely — this category collects only failure reviews, and the useful signal was the content of the complaints, not the average.

Why I eliminated each of the others

Sam's Club Checks
Genuinely equal on economics and arguably broader on stated business formats. Demoted only because its headline per-check figure traces to an older review and could not be corroborated for 2026. Straight swap if you're a Sam's member
Blank stock + print
Cheapest per check, no account data sitting in a cupboard, one stock for every account. Knocked out for now because it's a setup project — printer alignment, MICR toner, a test check — that your current check volume doesn't yet justify. Revisit above ~100 checks/month
Deluxe
The best security stock and the best-documented QuickBooks compatibility in the field. Knocked out on price: ~10× Costco at list, and only competitive when a 40%-off code happens to be live. Use it for a high-value account
Your bank
Zero data hand-off and same-day counter checks in an emergency. Knocked out as a routine source at 4–8× the price for paper printed to the same standard — unless your account tier includes checks free, which is the one thing to check before ordering anywhere. Emergencies and included-free tiers
Carousel / Walmart Checks
Clean reputations, no membership, very low prices. Knocked out for this purchase because they're personal-first: weaker on voucher/payroll business formats and per-check economics at business quantities. Right answer for a personal checkbook
Checks In The Mail / Checks Unlimited
Low advertised prices and real bulk business ranges. Knocked out by the elimination rule: surprise handling fees, unhonored discount codes and a pre-selected monitoring subscription mean the advertised price isn't the price. Only with a verified code and a careful checkout

What would change my mind

One fact: if your business checking already includes free or discounted checks, order through the bank for that account and ignore the price comparison entirely. Two others move it: if you hold no warehouse-club membership, the pick becomes a mainstream printer at $35–70 per 250 rather than paying $60+/year for a membership to save $40; and if check volume climbs past roughly 100 a month, blank stock plus in-house printing overtakes everything here.
